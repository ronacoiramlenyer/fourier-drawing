What does this formula mean?
z(t) = Σ rk exp(i(2πfkt + φk))

Treat the canvas as the complex plane: a point x + iy is one complex number z.

r eiθ is an arrow of length r pointing at angle θ. As t runs from 0 to 1, the angle 2πft + φ sweeps through f full turns, starting from φ.

Adding the arrows tip to tail gives the pen position. With whole-number frequencies every arrow is back at its start when t = 1, so the figure closes after exactly one cycle. Angles are shown in degrees here for readability.

How it works
  • Radius sets how strongly a circle contributes: the length of its arm.
  • Frequency is the number of rotations per cycle. Its sign sets the direction: positive turns counter-clockwise, negative turns clockwise, 0 stands still.
  • Phase is the angle the arm points at when t = 0.
  • The pen position is the sum of all the rotating arms, each one starting at the tip of the previous one.
  • Separate X and Y splits the work between two chains: the top chain’s tip sets the pen’s x, the left chain’s tip sets its y. Each axis is then a sum of cosine or sine waves, and unequal frequencies on the two axes give Lissajous figures.
  • Build with Circles lets you explore sums of circular motions directly: choose the circles, see the figure.
  • Trace a Drawing runs the other way. It samples your outline at evenly spaced points, then the Discrete Fourier Transform finds the circles (radius, frequency, phase) whose sum passes through those points.
  • Playback speed only changes how fast the clock t runs. The figure itself depends only on the circles.
