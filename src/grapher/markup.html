How to type math
y = 2x + 1A graph. You can also type just 2x + 1.
x^2Powers use ^. Write x^(1/2) with brackets for longer powers.
2x, 3sin(x)A number next to a letter means multiply.
sqrt(x), abs(x), |x|Square root and absolute value.
sin, cos, tanTrig functions, in radians unless you switch on degrees.
ln(x), log(x), exp(x)Natural log, base-10 log, and ex.
pi, eThe constants π and e.
a = 2A slider. Any new letter you use can become a slider.
f(x) = x^2 - 3Defines a function you can reuse: y = f(x - 2).
(2, 3)A point. (a, b) made of sliders can be dragged.
x = 3A vertical line, or any graph sideways: x = y^2.
x^2 + y^2 = 9Any equation in x and y, such as a circle.
(cos(t), sin(t))A parametric curve. t runs from 0 to 2π.
r = 1 + cos(theta)A polar curve. Type theta for θ.
sum(k, 1, n, 1/k)Adds up the expression for k = 1, 2, …, n.

Under each row you see the expression written out the way it was understood, so you can check that e^2x means e²·x and e^(2x) means e to the 2x.

How to read a graph
  • Every point has an address (x, y): x is how far right of the vertical axis, y is how far above the horizontal axis.
  • A graph of y = … is every point whose y equals the formula worked out at its x. Hover over a graph to read points from it.
  • Roots are where a graph crosses the x-axis, so y = 0 there.
  • The y-intercept is where it crosses the y-axis, at x = 0.
  • Peaks and valleys (local maximum and minimum) are where the graph turns around.
  • Intersections are where two graphs share a point: both formulas give the same y.
  • Moving around: drag the graph to move, scroll or pinch to zoom, and click a graph to list its key points.
